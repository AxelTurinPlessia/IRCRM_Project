\section{Bootsrapping}

\subsection{Constructing the discount curve}\label{subsec:1a}

Let \(t_0=\text{19 August 2026}\) be the spot date and let
\(P(t_0,T)\) denote the discount factor from \(t_0\) to maturity
\(T\), with \(P(t_0,t_0)=1\). For an accrual period \([A,B)\),
the ACT/360 fraction \(\alpha(A,B)\) is the number of calendar
days from \(A\) to \(B\) divided by 360.

The overnight SOFR quote is \(3.62\%\) per year. From the spot date
to \(T=\text{20 August 2026}\), the accrual fraction is \(1/360\).
Discounting the principal plus one day's simple interest gives
\[
P(t_0,T)=\frac{1}{1+0.0362\times\frac{1}{360}}
    \approx 0.9999.
\]
We assume zero futures convexity adjustment and use the implied
futures rate as the simple forward rate \(F(t_0;A,B)\) at spot
for the accrual period \([A,B)\). For SR1,
this follows the approximation of using the arithmetic
average of daily SOFR as a proxy for the simple forward rate.
The forward-rate identity gives
\[
1+\alpha(A,B)F(t_0;A,B)=\frac{P(t_0,A)}{P(t_0,B)},
\qquad
P(t_0,B)=\frac{P(t_0,A)}{1+\alpha(A,B)F(t_0;A,B)}.
\]
The modified August SR1 contract accrues from the spot date to
1 September 2026, giving a 13-day accrual period. We apply the
recursion over this period and then over the September SR1
period, from 1 September to 1 October 2026.

The first SR3 contract starts on 16 September 2026, halfway
between the two SR1 end dates. We interpolate the logarithms of
the corresponding discount factors:
\[
\log P(t_0,\text{16 Sep 2026})
  = \tfrac12\log P(t_0,\text{1 Sep 2026})
  + \tfrac12\log P(t_0,\text{1 Oct 2026}).
\]
Starting from this node, we apply the same forward-rate recursion
across the four SR3 reference quarters. Each quarter
spans 91 calendar days, and the final one ends on
15 September 2027.

Let \(U_0=t_0\), and let \(U_1,\ldots,U_{30}\) be the annual
OIS payment dates from the course schedule. We obtain the first
payment discount factor \(P(t_0,U_1)\) by interpolating simple
ACT/360 spot rates between the two SR3 maturities that bracket
\(U_1\). Part~\ref{subsec:1e} gives the calculation.

For a spot-starting OIS maturing at \(U_n\), write
\(\alpha_i=\alpha(U_{i-1},U_i)\) and let \(K_n\) be the par
fixed rate. The discounted floating payments telescope to
\(1-P(t_0,U_n)\). Equating the two legs gives
\[
K_n\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_i P(t_0,U_i)=1-P(t_0,U_n).
\]
Solving for the final discount factor yields
\[
P(t_0,U_n)
  = \frac{1-K_n\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\alpha_i P(t_0,U_i)}
         {1+K_n\alpha_n}.
\]
For an annual maturity without a quoted OIS rate, we interpolate
the par fixed rate linearly between the surrounding quoted
maturities, using actual calendar-day distances. We then apply
the recursion sequentially from \(U_2\) through \(U_{30}\).

The bootstrap yields discount factors at 38 future reset and payment
dates, in addition to \(P(t_0,t_0)=1\). 

\subsection{Repricing the market instruments}\label{subsec:1b}

We recompute each Table~\ref{tab:bootstrap-repricing} quote from the
discount factors \(P(t_0,T)\) using the previous pricing formulas. For the overnight
instrument and the futures, the implied annual rate over the
accrual period \([A,B)\) is
\[
\widehat r(A,B)
  = \frac{P(t_0,A)/P(t_0,B)-1}{\alpha(A,B)}.
\]
We convert this rate back to the original quote convention:
\(100\widehat r(A,B)\) for overnight SOFR and \(100(1-\widehat r(A,B))\)
for SR1 and SR3. We retain the modified August SR1 accrual period
and the futures assumptions used in part~\ref{subsec:1a}.

For an OIS maturing at \(U_n\), we recover the par fixed rate by
dividing the present value of the floating leg by the fixed-leg
annuity:
\[
\widehat K_n
  = \frac{1-P(t_0,U_n)}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_i P(t_0,U_i)}.
\]
The repriced OIS quote is \(100\widehat K_n\). Let \(\widehat q\)
and \(q_{\mathrm{market}}\) denote the repriced and market quotes
in their original quotation units. The signed quote error in
basis points is
\[
e_{\mathrm{bp}}=100\bigl(\widehat q-q_{\mathrm{market}}\bigr).
\]

\subsection{Zero-coupon bond prices and spot rates}\label{subsec:1c}

We take the discount factors \(P(t_0,T_i)\) from the bootstrapped
curve at the maturities listed in Table~2 of the project instructions.
Under ACT/365, the time to maturity is
\[
\delta(t_0,T_i)
  = \frac{\text{number of calendar days from }t_0\text{ to }T_i}{365}.
\]
The continuously compounded spot rate \(y(t_0,T_i)\) satisfies
\(P(t_0,T_i)=\exp[-y(t_0,T_i)\delta(t_0,T_i)]\), so
\[
y(t_0,T_i)=-\frac{\log P(t_0,T_i)}{\delta(t_0,T_i)}.
\]

\subsection{Interpolated discount, spot, and forward curves}\label{subsec:1d}

We use the ten discount factors from part~\ref{subsec:1c} and the spot
normalization \(P(t_0,t_0)=1\). Let \(\tau=\delta(t_0,T)\) denote
time to maturity under ACT/365. Between consecutive nodes with
times \(\tau_i\) and \(\tau_{i+1}\), we interpolate the log
discount factor as
\[
\log P(t_0,T)
  = (1-w)\log P(t_0,T_i) + w\log P(t_0,T_{i+1}),
\qquad
w=\frac{\tau-\tau_i}{\tau_{i+1}-\tau_i}.
\]
We evaluate the interpolated curve at the 10,950 daily maturities
\(\tau=m/365\), for \(m=1,\ldots,365\times30\).

The continuously compounded spot rate is
\(y(t_0,T)=-\log P(t_0,T)/\tau\). The instantaneous forward rate is
the negative slope of the log discount factor with respect to
\(\tau\). Within each interpolation interval, it is therefore
constant and given by
\[
f(t_0,T)
  = \frac{\log P(t_0,T_i)-\log P(t_0,T_{i+1})}
         {\tau_{i+1}-\tau_i}.
\]
This gives the forward curve its step shape, with changes in slope
at the interpolation nodes.

Figure~\ref{fig:bootstrap-curves} shows the discount curve and
the two rate curves. The crosses mark the ten discount factors
from part~\ref{subsec:1c}. The final cross is at \(30.0274\) ACT/365 years,
so the discount line extends to that node beyond the 30-year grid.

\subsection{The first annual OIS payment date}\label{subsec:1e}

The 19 August 2027 row in Table~\ref{tab:zero-coupon-bonds} corresponds to \(U_1\), the first annual OIS payment date.
Table~\ref{tab:bootstrap-repricing} gives OIS quotes starting at the 2Y maturity, so
\(P(t_0,U_1)\) must be obtained from the futures segment.

The date \(U_1\) lies between the last two SR3 maturities,
\(A=\text{16 June 2027}\) and \(B=\text{15 September 2027}\).
The SR3 bootstrap gives \(P(t_0,A)\approx 0.9682\) and
\(P(t_0,B)\approx 0.9585\). Using the full-precision discount
factors, we calculate the simple ACT/360 spot rates:
\[
L(A)=\frac{1/P(t_0,A)-1}{301/360}\approx 3.9296\%,
\qquad
L(B)=\frac{1/P(t_0,B)-1}{392/360}\approx 3.9767\%.
\]
The interval from \(A\) to \(B\) spans 91 days. Since \(U_1\)
is 64 days after \(A\) and 27 days before \(B\), linear
interpolation of the simple spot rates gives
\[
L(U_1)=\frac{27}{91}L(A)+\frac{64}{91}L(B)
      \approx 3.9627\%.
\]
There are 365 days from \(t_0\) to \(U_1\), so
\[
P(t_0,U_1)=\frac{1}{1+(365/360)L(U_1)}\approx 0.9614.
\]
Using ACT/365 for reporting, the time to maturity is
\(1.0000\) year and the continuously compounded spot rate is
\(3.9391\%\).

With \(P(t_0,U_1)\) known, the 2Y par quote determines
\(P(t_0,U_2)\approx 0.9233\). The first payment's discount factor
also enters the fixed-leg annuity for every longer OIS.
